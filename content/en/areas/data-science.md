## Overview {#overview}

Data Science is an interdisciplinary field concerned with extracting
information and knowledge from complex structured and unstructured data. It
combines mathematical statistics, scientific methods, data-driven algorithms,
scientific computing and computer systems to address scientific and practical
problems.

## Research Themes {#research-themes}

CENTAUR studies the theoretical and applied dimensions of statistical data
science through the following research themes.

### Data Analytics and Visualization

This theme develops methods for the exploratory analysis of heterogeneous
datasets to identify patterns, anomalies and structural behaviour. Research
also examines graphical representations, including interactive dashboards and
multidimensional visualizations, that make complex analytical results easier
to interpret and use in scientific and organizational decisions.

### Statistical Learning and High-Dimensional Data Analysis

This theme combines statistical inference with computational algorithms to
develop predictive and explanatory models with guarantees concerning accuracy,
stability and interpretability. Research studies empirical risk and
generalization from finite training samples to unseen data. It includes
regularization and sparsity methods, dimensionality reduction, manifold
learning, resampling and model validation.

### Computational Statistics and Optimization

This theme develops numerical tools for implementing complex statistical
models at scale. Research includes optimization of constrained objective
functions, methods for intractable probability distributions, stochastic
optimization, simulation, and the design of deterministic and stochastic
algorithms.

### Scientific Computing

Scientific computing combines mathematical modelling, numerical analysis and
software design to address large-scale scientific and engineering problems.
Research examines high-performance implementation, data integrity and
algorithms for parallel architectures, with emphasis on reproducibility,
numerical stability and scalability.
